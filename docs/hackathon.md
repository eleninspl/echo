# Qloo Agentic Hackathon 2026

Online, on Devpost. The brief: "Agents are getting smarter, but they're culturally blind." Build an AI agent, or an app powered by one, that uses Qloo's taste data to do something it couldn't do without it. In Qloo's words, if the project would work the same without Qloo, it is the wrong project.

## Prizes

$15,000, $6,000 and $4,000 in cash. One judge, an angel investor, will also personally invest $25,000 in a favourite entry. About 1,590 people registered.

## What a submission needs

- **A live, hosted demo** that judges can use from start to finish. Anything that only runs on a laptop doesn't qualify.
- **Public source code** under an open-source licence (the repo has MIT and goes public at submission).
- **A written description** of what it does and what makes it powered by Qloo. No video required.

The submission guide also asks teams to show how a request turns into a result and to state the project's limits. Judges look at responsible data handling and whether the work can be reproduced.

## How it is judged

Four criteria, equal weight. Ties go to whoever scores higher on the first one.

| Criterion | What the judges ask |
| --- | --- |
| Technical implementation | How thoroughly and skilfully does it use Qloo? Is it real, working, non-trivial work? |
| Design | Is it a complete, coherent product, not just a proof of concept? |
| Potential impact | Does it make a specific, believable case for a real problem and a real audience, and does the demo solve it? |
| Quality of the idea | Is it a creative, non-obvious use of Qloo, and does the team understand the problem space? |

## Judges

Six, by role:

- An angel investor **who hosts a podcast** (the one making the $25,000 investment)
- Qloo's chief technology officer
- An OpenAI board member with a legal background
- An owner of major sports clubs
- A comedian and actor
- A Live Nation strategy executive

The panel leans towards investors, entertainment and live events.

## What won last year

Qloo LLM Hackathon, July to August 2025: 343 projects, 4 winners. Three of the four were decision tools for businesses (the grand prize was a market analysis agent). None of the plain consumer recommenders won.

## Qloo's rules

- Results describe groups, not individuals. Never present a match as a probability about one person.
- Don't use results to infer anyone's identity, health, finances, political views or other sensitive traits.
- Don't send personal data such as location histories to Qloo.
- No bulk scraping and no rebuilding an unrelated database from Qloo calls. Usage limits aren't published and every call draws on the same quota.
